## Why is this study being done?
Video calls with doctors are now common in India, but they record no vital signs. A camera can
pick up the tiny colour changes in facial skin with each heartbeat and estimate heart rate. Such
camera methods are known to work less well on darker skin and in dim light. We want to test our
recording set-up and analysis on volunteers of every skin tone before a larger study.

## What will happen if I take part?
The visit takes about 25–30 minutes in our laboratory.
1. We ask a few questions about your health (to check that the study is suitable for you), your age,
   sex, and things that can affect a camera (beard, spectacles, make-up, bindi).
2. One or two trained researchers compare your cheek with a printed skin-tone scale (the Monk
   Skin Tone scale). You will also choose the shade you think matches you.
3. A small clip is placed on one fingertip. It measures your pulse with light, like the clip used in
   hospitals. It does not hurt.
4. A webcam records your face (and, if used, a phone camera) while you:
   - hold a grey card next to your cheek for 30 seconds,
   - sit still and then nod gently for 30 seconds,
   - rest quietly for 3 minutes,
   - answer ten everyday health questions as in a video consultation (3 minutes),
   - count backwards in steps of 7 or 13 for 2 minutes (mildly challenging, on purpose).
   This is done once with a desk lamp and once with ordinary room light.

## Are there any risks or discomforts?
The risks are minimal. Counting backwards can feel a little stressful; this passes within minutes.
Some people find being video-recorded uncomfortable. You can stop at any time without giving a reason.

## Are there any benefits?
There is no direct benefit to you. The study will help make camera-based health measurements work
fairly for people of every skin tone.

## Will I learn my heart rate or any health result?
No. The values are research measurements from a system under development. They are not medical
results, and we will not give them to you as health information. If you have any health concern,
please see a doctor.

## What happens to my data?
- Your face video is personal data. It is stored only on an encrypted computer and an encrypted
  backup drive in the laboratory, never on a cloud service, and only the study team can open it.
- Your name and contact details appear only on the paper consent form, kept locked and separately.
  Everything else is labelled with a code (for example P07).
- You can choose (on the consent form) whether your video may be shared with other researchers
  under a signed data-use agreement, or used only within SVNIT.
- Numbers derived from the video (colour averages, heart-rate estimates) and your skin-tone ratings
  may be published without your name or face.
- Data handling follows the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the ICMR National Ethical
  Guidelines (2017). Video is kept for ____ years and then deleted.

## Do I have to take part? Can I withdraw?
Taking part is voluntary. You may withdraw at any time, also after the session, by contacting the
PI and quoting your code; your video will then be deleted. Data already included in published
anonymous summaries cannot be removed from them.

# 1. Reviewer's verdict on the rewritten draft

*Written in the voice of an expert-committee member who has to rank this against other early-career proposals in Engineering Sciences. The first review (9 November) found 3 critical, 14 major and 9 minor problems; this is the verdict after they were fixed. The fixes are listed in `CHANGES.md` under [review].*

**Summary of the proposal.** A 36-month, ₹33.67 lakh measurement-science project that records 150 Indian adults (45 analysable per skin-tone stratum) with colorimeter-measured skin tone, 1 kHz ECG, four cameras, two lights and a seven-level compression ladder; audits ten rPPG methods and decomposes their error; develops a method with capture-side exposure control and a reliability gate; and tests camera HRV against ECG. A pre-funding pilot on Indian volunteers has run the recording and analysis pipeline end to end.

**Strengths.**

1. A clear, nationally relevant question (eSanjeevani scale, a documented optical equity failure in pulse oximetry, no Indian dataset with instrument-measured skin tone), scoped honestly as measurement science with no device claim.
2. Unusually rigorous design for an early-career proposal: one primary endpoint per hypothesis, criteria simulated *as worded*, type-I error checked, decision rules that name an inconclusive outcome, abstention bias handled by scoring all windows, pre-registration on OSF.
3. The error-budget design (lossless reference camera plus consumer devices plus a compression ladder within the same person) answers *why* errors differ, not only *whether*.
4. Working, released software and a pilot on the target population reduce execution risk; every number in section 5 is generated by code and the build refuses unmeasured numbers.
5. Budget is itemised, justified line by line and well under the cap; the storage line is now sized to the data.

**Weaknesses a committee will still raise.**

1. *The pilot's skin tone is camera-estimated, not colorimeter-measured, and its reference is a finger PPG, not ECG.* → Section 5.2 says so and treats the pilot as feasibility only. Check that no sentence in 5.2 reads as a test of H1–H2, and that the camera ITA°–Monk agreement is shown next to any skin-tone number.
2. *Novelty of the method is incremental* (POS + small CNN + group-DRO + conformal gate). → The proposal claims novelty for the combination with measured skin tone, compression and ECG, and for the per-stratum reliability audit, and every step is ablated. The in-silico rehearsal found no benefit of FiLM or group-DRO; the proposal must not hint otherwise.
3. *Recruiting 50 people with ITA° > 28 in Surat* (≈15% of Indian adults in one colorimeter study). → Screening of up to 390, a pre-registered tertile rule, continuous ITA° for H1–H2. The pilot's Monk distribution is the best evidence; if few lighter volunteers came, say so.
4. *H1's effect is borrowed from a Fitzpatrick-labelled study* and re-anchored by assumption. → Section 6 shows power under the conservative anchor (0.83 at 45), a wider spread (0.64) and the October effect (0.95); the month-10 re-check can add up to 5 per stratum. A reviewer may still ask why not 60 per stratum: the answer is cost and the narrower Indian ITA° range, stated.
5. *H3 may be under-powered* if the true gain is 0.5 bpm (power 0.50). → Stated openly; H3 is reported with its CI either way.
6. *H5's anchor is weak* (35 white participants). → Power shown at ICC 0.75 (0.92) and 0.70 (0.50); the criterion is a lower bound, not a point estimate.
7. *Healthy volunteers, not patients.* → Scope statement; the step test widens the HR range; a clinical project is named as the next step.
8. *Single PI, one fellow.* → Project Associate for the recording year; the recorder and analysis already exist.

**Score I would give (out of 10):** significance 8; novelty 7; methodology 8 (rigorous, but the H1 anchor and the weak H5 anchor will be probed); feasibility 8 with real pilot numbers in 5.2 (5–6 if 5.2 is empty or says "pending"); PI competence 7 (rPPG record is thin; the pilot and toolkit are the answer); budget 9. **Likely outcome:** competitive if section 5.2 carries real pilot numbers, the ethics reference is filled and the eligibility items are clean; weak otherwise. The single largest lever left is section 5.2.

# 3. Questions the committee may ask at a presentation

1. *What is the single most important number from your pilot?* — The usable-session count and the synchronisation spread (feasibility), then the coverage of SAMA's shown intervals on people it never saw.
2. *Why should India fund a dataset when Vital Videos has 893 people?* — Vital Videos labels skin tone on the Fitzpatrick scale rather than with an instrument, has no ECG-grade HRV and no controlled compression; SAMA-150 measures skin tone with an instrument in the population that will use eSanjeevani.
3. *How will you know your camera ITA° is good enough to replace a colorimeter?* — We will not assume it: SAMA-150 measures both and reports their agreement; the pilot already reports camera ITA° against Monk ratings.
4. *Why 50 per stratum and not 40?* — Because the H1 effect was re-anchored conservatively to the Indian ITA° range; at 36 analysable H1 power is 0.74, at 45 it is 0.83.
5. *What happens if compression erases the pulse at 500 kbps?* — Then prediction A or a floor effect holds, and the actionable advice is to measure on the phone before compression; the ladder's higher levels still give the dose–response.
6. *Is the reliability gate useful if it withholds most readings for darker skin?* — That is exactly what the per-stratum audit is for; withholding is reported, never hidden, and the useful-yield measure shows the cost.
7. *What will you do in months 1–2?* — Equipment purchase, ethics for the main study, OSF pre-registration, fellow recruitment; the toolkit already runs.
